### Cross-species comparison of the protein sequences

The proteins of human, *Canis lupus familiaris* and giant panda are 148
residues long (numbering includes the 18-residue signal peptide) and align
without gaps. Of the 148 positions, 115 (77.7 %) are identical in the three
species. Pairwise identity is 80.4 % for human–dog, 80.4 % for human–panda and
93.2 % for dog–panda, so dog and panda are closer to each other than to human.

**Conserved sequences.** Several stretches are identical in the three species.
The longest is positions 69–89 (STDYGIFQINSRYWCNDGKTP, 21 aa); others are
21–31 (FERCELARTLK, 11 aa), 56–64 (YNTRATNYN), 110–118 (AVACAKRVV) and
123–131 (GIRAWVAWR). The two catalytic residues (Glu53 and Asp71, UniProt
P61626 active sites; Glu35 and Asp53 in mature numbering) are conserved, and
so are all eight cysteines (positions 24, 48, 83, 95, 99, 113, 134 and 146).
Conservation is highest in positions 61–90 (86.7 % identity) and in the region
corresponding to the β-domain (positions 57–103, 85.1 %).

**Sequences that change.** 33 positions (22.3 %) differ among the three
species. All are substitutions; there are no insertions or deletions. At 23 of
them dog and panda share the same residue, which differs from human; at 4
human and panda coincide, at 4 human and dog coincide, and at 2 positions
(6 and 55) the three residues are different. The signal peptide (positions
1–18) is less conserved (72.2 % identity) than the mature protein (78.5 %).

**Disease-associated position.** The residue affected by the disease variant,
Ile74 (I56 in mature numbering), is identical in the three species and lies
within the longest conserved block (positions 69–89). The variant
(p.Ile74Thr) introduces a threonine that is not found at this position in any
of the three species.

**Protein vs CDS.** When compared per unit (identical residues vs identical
codons), the species are more similar at the protein level, because 31 of the
64 codons that vary are synonymous.
