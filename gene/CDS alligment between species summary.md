### Cross-species comparison of the CDS

The CDS of human, *Canis lupus familiaris* and giant panda are 447 nt long, in
frame, and align without gaps. Of the 447 positions, 362 (81.0 %) are
identical in the three species. Pairwise identity is 83.9 % for human–dog,
84.1 % for human–panda and 93.5 % for dog–panda, so dog and panda are closer
to each other than to human.

**Conserved sequences.** Several long stretches are identical in the three
species: c.46–57 (GTCCAGGGCAAG, encoding VQGK), c.59–74 and c.77–94 (FERCELARTLK
region, with only two synonymous differences at c.75 and c.76), c.211–263
(DYGIFQINSRYWCNDGK region, with a single synonymous difference at c.228 in
dog), c.337–354 (encoding AKRVV), c.376–393 and the last 13 nucleotides,
c.435–447. The longest fully conserved block spans c.229–263 (35 nt).

**Sequences that change.** 85 positions (19.0 %) differ among the three
species. All are substitutions; there are no insertions or deletions. At 56 of
them dog and panda share the same base, which differs from human. Variation is
higher in the signal peptide region (c.1–54, 24 % of positions) and in
c.394–434 (34 %). Substitutions concentrate at the third codon position
(41 of 85, vs. 30 at the first and 14 at the second), and of the 64 codons that
vary, 31 are synonymous and 33 change the amino acid.

**Disease-associated position.** The position affected by the disease variant
(c.221, T in the human WT CDS) is identical in dog and panda, and the whole
codon 74 (ATA, Ile) is conserved in the three species. It lies within the
fully conserved stretch c.211–263. The variant (c.221T>C, p.Ile74Thr)
introduces a base that is not found at this position in any of the three
species.
